What coaching can do here

A restructure takes a lot out of your hands.
This hour is about what's still in them.

Coaching won't change a restructure, and I won't pretend a hard situation is an opportunity in disguise.

What it can do is give you an hour where the thinking is about you: what you want, what you're assuming, and which choices are open to you in the circumstances you've got.

01

You choose what we work on.

A decision about severance, a conversation with your head of department, an application you keep not sending.

02

I listen and say what I notice.

I won't hand you a list of jobs for people leaving academia. I'll ask questions and offer observations for you to take or leave.

03

You leave with a next step.

A decision to test, a conversation to have, or one manageable thing to try. Small is fine.

Before we talk

A few things
you might be wondering.

Nothing is confirmed yet. Is it too early?

No. Waiting can be the hardest part. You can use a session to think about what you'd want in each outcome, so the decision isn't made in a rush when the letter arrives.

Will you tell me what I could do outside higher education?

I won't give you a list. We can look at what you've done, what you'd want more or less of, and how you could find out whether a direction suits you.

Is it confidential?

I don't work for your university, and I won't share what you tell me with your employer.

Can my university pay?

Some universities fund career support during a restructure. If yours does, email me and we'll sort out the paperwork.

Is coaching the right kind of support?

My coaching focuses on career decisions, goals and action. It doesn't provide mental health treatment, legal advice or advice on the redundancy process itself. Your union rep or Acas is the place for questions about your rights.
