For people starting out

First job not going how you hoped?

A few months in, and it isn't what you pictured. The feedback stings more than it should. Everyone else seems to know things nobody told you. You've started wondering whether you're cut out for this.

If that's where you are, it's worth knowing you're not the only one, and that it doesn't automatically mean the problem is you.

Managers and new starters often see it differently

In research published by the Chartered Management Institute in July 2026, managers said young people starting work most often lacked resilience, professionalism, clear communication and the ability to act on feedback. Only 6% said young starters showed strong self-management skills. In a YouGov poll for the same report, 45% of 18 to 24 year-olds felt they'd arrived well equipped. The two groups were asked slightly different questions, so treat that as a rough comparison.

Seven in ten young people in that poll believed employers were biased against their generation. And 78% felt their education had leaned too heavily on theory rather than the practical side of doing a job.

None of that tells you what's going on in your job. It does tell you the gap between how you feel you're doing and how you're being seen is common, and it runs both ways.

Three things that get mixed up

When a first job feels hard, it tends to land as one big verdict: I'm not good enough. Usually it's a mix of a few different things.

Things you don't know yet. How to write an email to someone senior. When to ask and when to work it out. What “quick look” means to your manager. These are learnable, and not knowing them in month three is normal. They feel personal because nobody tells you they're unwritten.

There's also the question of who you can ask. If every small query has to go to the person deciding how you're doing, even “Where's last month's version?” can feel like a daft question. Is there someone on the team you'd feel comfortable asking to show you how they do it?

Feeling like an outsider is part of this too. Understanding the work and feeling accepted by the people around you are separate things, and the second often takes longer. “I don't belong here yet” can easily turn into “I chose the wrong career”. Those are very different conclusions.

Things the setup isn't giving you. Clear expectations. Feedback you can do something with. Someone with time to show you. If those are missing, you can work very hard and still feel behind. That isn't a personal failing, even if it feels like one.

A real mismatch. Sometimes the work, the place or the people don't suit you. That's worth knowing too. Finding it out early is useful, not a failure.

From the inside, all three feel much the same. That's why they're worth pulling apart.

A few questions worth sitting with

I'm not going to give you a list of tips. You've probably read plenty. These are questions that tend to be more useful than advice:

  • When something goes wrong, what do you tell yourself it says about you?
  • Which of the things you're struggling with could you learn in six months, and which wouldn't change however long you stayed?
  • What would you need to know from your manager to stop guessing? Have you asked?
  • If you got good at this, would you want to keep doing it?

You don't need to answer them all today. Even noticing which one makes you wince a bit tells you something.
